## Verdict

Stop patching symptoms. The recurring blowout/repeat/stuck/rest-unfairness cases are not four bugs but one causal chain — corrected for honesty after the critique: a pvna-blind tier/required/fairness layer MANUFACTURES a skill-bimodal owed pool (RC2, RC5); a scoring system that prices balance last and hard-deletes the balanced split via FIVE INFINITY gates plus the unaudited avoid/group modalities AMPLIFIES it (RC3, RC7); greedy irreversible per-court allocation CONVERTS it into a served defect for MULTI-court fills (RC1) while the single-court rolling case is converted instead by cross-request live-set composition plus the same generator layer; and ~16 fragmented repairs merely RELOCATE the failure (RC4) — the mechanical reason eight targeted patches produced eight new cases. The lead's 'greedy is THE shared root' was overstated: greedy is the proven converter for the multi-court portion, but it explicitly does not operate within a single-court request, so the generator layer (RC2/RC5) and the constraint modalities (RC3/RC7) are the true roots of the dominant single-court case. The lead also under-inventoried the gates (five, not two), missed avoid.ts entirely (avoid-partner Infinity is a 5th split-deleter, avoid-opponent 300x pvna), over-scoped #23 as a standalone live-stuck root (the live path is fallback-protected except under the budget-skip guard, so #24 is the real live-stuck mechanism), and asserted both 'weight-tuning is dead' (true only for depleted pools) and 'single-court blowouts are optimal, surface don't fix' (unproven) without evidence. Recommended path is three-track: (1) immediately ship the cheap generator + budget fixes — pvna-aware/capped required-selection, gated fairness MUST_PLAY and tolerance relax, reserved rescue budget, and relaxed fallback-skip — which cuts recurrence in both the multi- and single-court cases within weeks; (2) close the audit blind spots — audit avoid/group modalities, measure rolling-state drift magnitude and strict-pass fallthrough before committing to the RC5 rebuild or declaring tolerance mis-calibrated; then (3) invest in a bounded synchronous joint 2-3 court allocator for the multi-court portion, but only after resolving #70 (plan-target reverting the pull-from-bench repairs) and fixing the generator layer, or it inherits an impossible required set. Full CP-SAT re-architecture is not warranted. Weight-tuning is a partial, not dead, lever — it changes the selected solution in the non-depleted avoid-pair/repeat-3 slice — but for depleted/bimodal pools the constraint is the reachable solution set, and only fixing the generator + changing the partition enlarges it.


## Root causes (chuỗi nhân quả, KHÔNG phải 4 bug rời)


### RC1 — Greedy sequential per-court allocation with irreversible commits and no joint re-partition (the shared CONVERTER for the multi-court class)
buildSuggestedMatchPayloads fills one court per suggestNextMatch call (courts forced to 1, suggest.ts:875) in fixed ASCENDING court-index order (verified live-preview.ts:4184-4188: openCourtIdxs built 0..capacity, first not-yet-in-round chosen) and permanently commits each court's 4 players to batchBusyIds before the next court is solved (verified live-preview.ts:5106-5111). No path re-cuts the joint partition, so the LAST-filled court mathematically inherits the earlier courts' residue: bimodal residue -> forced blowout, stale residue -> forced 3rd meeting, <4 residue -> empty/stuck court. The two mitigations that could spread owed players both early-return at remainingCourtsInRound<=1 (verified live-preview.ts:1703 selectRequiredIdsForCourt slice; 1739 deferLowViabilityRequiredIdsForCourt unchanged) — i.e. balancing is switched OFF for the final court of every full-board fill. HONEST SCOPE CORRECTION (conceding the critique): the 'last court inherits residue' mechanism is a MULTI-court phenomenon. On the dominant single-court rolling refill (effectiveCount===1) there is exactly one court per request, so this intra-request mechanism does NOT operate; the blowout there is driven by (a) which players are currently live/busy — a cross-request artifact of match-completion order — and (b) the generator layer (RC2). So RC1 is the proven CONVERTER for the multi-court portion, not the unifying root of the single-court case.

_bug-classes:_ blowout, repeat-3, court-stuck


### RC2 — The generator layer (tier + required + fairness overrides) manufactures a skill-bimodal owed pool BEFORE allocation runs
classifyPlayer is entirely pvna/skill-blind (verified classify.ts:52-78, no pvna reference; skill enters only as the last tie-break in select.ts:53). mustPlayAt is a hard-coded constant 1 while mustRestAt scales up with bench depth (classify.ts:31-37), so entering MUST_PLAY is trivial (rest once) and leaving via MUST_REST is hard on a large bench — the owed pool inflates every round. The live/full-board required set re-implements MUST_PLAY inline and is also pvna-blind (verified suggest.ts:1042-1053). The fairness corrector force-sets MUST_PLAY for underplayed/match_count players with NO viability check (verified corrector.ts:41-47), and rest_violation relaxes GLOBAL pvna_tolerance +0.15 uncapped/ungated board-wide while also forcing MUST_PLAY (verified corrector.ts:74-80). The net product is a uniformly-tiered, skill-lopsided required group handed to pairing as players that MUST be seated together — a blowout by construction. Self-reinforcing loop: engine benches a PVNA outlier to avoid a blowout -> outlier becomes underplayed -> fairness forces MUST_PLAY -> the only foursome that seats them is a blowout. This layer is what makes the single-court case a blowout even though RC1's intra-request mechanism doesn't apply there — the depleted/bimodal idle pool is itself a product of prior generator decisions.

_bug-classes:_ blowout, repeat-3, court-stuck, rest-unfairness


### RC3 — Scoring scale + FIVE hard INFINITY gates price balance below everything and can eliminate the only balanced split
In the additive scoreMatch, pvnaDiff carries weight 1 — the SMALLEST split-differentiating term — while recent-repeat is 28/80/80, severe-repeat 50/pair, group_bonus 6, gender 4/2, partner_repeat 3, avoid-opponent 300 (verified score.ts:42, 63-69, 637-641, 650-661). Once tolerance gates relax/open, split selection systematically trades team balance away to dodge a repeat/gender/group-split/avoid — avoiding a repeat or separating an avoid-pair structurally out-prices avoiding a blowout. CRITIQUE-DRIVEN CORRECTION: the lead cited only TWO INFINITY gates; there are FIVE that can each delete the only balanced split of a bimodal foursome in strict passes (verified score.ts:583 intra-gap, 598 tolerance, 601 hasRecentGroupRematch, 608 hasRepeatOverflow, 616 avoid-partner). The balanced-split-deletion mechanism is therefore BROADER and more robust than #1 claimed. Note #1's cited bestTeamSplit path (pair.ts:80-82) is DEAD/test-only (verified: callers only in pair.ts def, tests, one trace script — confirming #40); the LIVE deleter is the strict A/C suggest passes and bestPartitioning's early stages, which call the same gated scoreMatch. Partner/opponent repeats are double-counted (structured group-aware term AND non-group-aware recent term), the two batch scorers rank pvna-vs-intra in OPPOSITE directions, and getMatchGroupKey ignores team boundaries.

_bug-classes:_ blowout, repeat-3, court-stuck


### RC4 — Fragmented multi-objective repair architecture that cannot converge and has coverage gaps/conflicts
After the greedy fill, quality is patched by ~16 independent local optimizers, each defending a DIFFERENT metric with its OWN scoring function and per-metric guards, run in a fixed sequence over a partition none of them can re-cut. Three visibly different scorers (scorePayloadRepeatRepair 1000/180/120..., a participation comparator, a PVNA-first 2-opt swap) plus scoreEarlyBatchQuality (which weights intra 40x pvna, opposite to scoreMatch) hill-climb conflicting objectives, so tightening one pass's threshold only relocates the failure to the metric the next reported case hits — the mechanical reason point-fixes never converge. Coverage gaps compound it: blowout pool repair is strict-equal gated to degraded_reason==='blowout' so it skips the worst 'both' courts AND the moderate (tolerance,1.5] band; single-court repeat-3 gets no pool repair; participation/rest repair is disabled on any live board; blowout repair benches players with no owed-rank guard; and repairs never recompute degraded_reason, persisting stale wait-rescue banners. UNRESOLVED CONFLICT the critique surfaced: an active rolling invariant target silently reverts BOTH pull-from-bench repairs (live-preview.ts:5247-5250) because they change the selected-player key — so a future joint/plan solver that sets rollingPlanTarget would resurrect exactly the blowout/repeat cases these repairs fix (#48/#70). This must be resolved BEFORE joint allocation ships.

_bug-classes:_ blowout, repeat-3, rest-unfairness, court-stuck


### RC5 — Rolling-lane state reconstruction drift makes rest debt invisible and pools temporally skewed (mechanism strong, magnitude UNMEASURED)
Logical rounds and per-player fairness state are reconstructed in four separate, mutually-inconsistent places (live-rounds.ts, the completion RPC, the on-demand repair RPC, live-cycle-rows.ts). The engine's SessionState takes matches_played/consecutive_rest/consecutive_play verbatim from a possibly-stale DB snapshot (verified state.ts:256-259) while recomputing rounds_available/current_round from reconstructed rounds — a mixed-source model with no reconciliation, and the live suggest path never calls the repair RPC. cycle_no/round_no is a PER-LANE depth counter, so during rolling single-court refills a round bucket rarely reaches court-count matches; the completion RPC's round-complete gate then never fires and benched players' consecutive_rest never increments — rest debt becomes invisible to the tier/priority layers, producing progressive rest-starvation and distorted required pools that feed RC2. HONEST CAVEAT (conceding the critique): the causal MECHANISM is code-verified, but the MAGNITUDE of drift in a real session and whether corrected counters would change tier/selection outcomes is NOT demonstrated by any replay. The high severity rests on a mechanism argument; a drifted-session replay diffing tiers/priority under raw-snapshot vs repair-RPC recomputation is required to confirm behavioral divergence.

_bug-classes:_ rest-unfairness, blowout, repeat-3

Independent of the partition problem, suggestNextRound builds requiredPlayerIds from every rester (consecutive_rest>=1) as an UNCAPPED hard constraint with no exhaustive fallback of its own, so a bench larger than the slot count makes every candidate skipped_required -> false NO_VALID_MATCH (#23). CRITIQUE-DRIVEN RE-SCOPE (verified): the LIVE single-court path is NOT directly exposed to #23 — suggestNextMatch wraps suggestNextRound and, whenever the top alternative is missing or relaxed, runs suggestNextMatchExhaustiveFallback which caps required to 4 (verified suggest.ts:918-925, 1067-1075). So #23 HARD-sticks only on (a) direct suggestNextRound callers that lack this net (alternatives.ts audit, full-round generation) and (b) the live path ONLY when budget pressure trips the remainingRuntimeMs<=100 guard that SKIPS the fallback (verified suggest.ts:929). That budget interaction is the TRUE live-stuck mechanism and ties #23 to #24: at the default 1000ms budget the regular-search deadline equals the overall deadline (zero reserve, suggest.ts:583-588), starving the forced-rescue pass precisely in the large/slow pools it exists for. Forced ids can also be dropped by the >4 cap then filtered out (#26), and courtsToSuggest=0 still yields a phantom court (#27). These convert to 'stuck' complaints that look like the allocation bug but are constraint/budget defects.

### RC7 — Constraint modalities (avoid-pairs, group-play, gender) act as UNAUDITED generators AND hard gates that shrink the feasible pool (critique gap, partially verified)
The lead audit had ZERO findings on avoid.ts despite it being a first-class modality. ## Scoring assessment

The scoring measures broadly the right axes but is NOT internally consistent, and the per-area 'scoring is largely sound' framing is too generous. VERIFIED contradictions: (1) In scoreMatch (score.ts:650-661) pvnaDiff*weights.pvna=1 is the smallest split-differentiating term while recent-repeat is 28/80/80 (score.ts:63-69), SEVERE_REPEAT_PAIR_PENALTY=50 (score.ts:42), and AVOID_OPPONENT_PENALTY=300 (score.ts:637-641, verified) — a single recent overlap-3 (80), exact-rematch (152), or avoid-opponent (300) buys essentially ANY blowout, so avoiding a repeat/separating an avoid-pair structurally out-prices avoiding a blowout. (2) FIVE hard INFINITY gates, not two, can delete the only balanced split of a bimodal foursome (verified score.ts:583 intra, 598 tolerance, 601 recentGroupRematch, 608 repeatOverflow, 616 avoid-partner) — the lead's #1 gate inventory was incomplete; the balanced-split-deletion mechanism is broader and more robust than claimed. (3) Two batch scorers rank pvna-vs-intra in OPPOSITE directions: scoreMatch makes pvna ~6.7x the intra soft term, scoreEarlyBatchQuality weights maxIntra 40x maxPvna (#7). (4) Partner/opponent repeats are double-counted — a structured group-aware term (3/1.5) AND a non-group-aware recent term (28/4) both fire for the same event, and the recent term ignores the intra-group exemption the structured term honors (#3, #4), so the engine simultaneously calls same-group repeats 'free' and 'worth 152'. (5) getMatchGroupKey ignores the A/B split, so re-mixing the same four with fresh partners is falsely scored as an exact rematch +80 (#5). (6) The burden tie-break window (±3) absorbs a full extra partner repeat or a ~3.0 pvna gap (#29, #36). (7) On the fairness side the rest SCORE flags any 2-round rest as a violation (metrics.ts:500, >1), stricter than the bench-scaled detector (2/3/4) and than what the engine enforces by design (#60); the churn multiplier scales the whole rest score not the penalty (#61); the audit panel mislabels diversity /20 and /15 vs the real 17/18 (#62). CRITIQUE CORRECTION on 'weight-tuning is dead': that verdict is TRUE for depleted pools but OVERSTATED as universal. For the non-trivial slice where an avoid-pair (300) or repeat-3 (50) exists AND a balanced non-blowout alternative exists at small penalty cost, re-normalizing weights WOULD change the reachable-and-selected solution. The decisive point still holds for depleted/bimodal pools: a perfectly-calibrated scorer cannot construct a balanced foursome from a depleted pool, and the strict gates actively delete the balanced split when one exists — but scoring is a partial lever, not a dead one. UNVERIFIED (flagged): whether pvna_tolerance=0.5 / PREFERRED_INTRA=0.75 / INTRA_LIMIT=1.0 are calibrated to the actual PVNA numeric scale is not measured; if 0.5 is systematically too tight, near-universal fallthrough to relaxed stages would promote 'tolerance-too-tight' to a competing root — this needs instrumentation of strict-pass failure rates.

### meta-architecture (7)

- **[high] Greedy per-court fill in fixed court-index order is the shared structural root of blowout+repeat+stuck** — `live-preview.ts:4168-4375 (fill loop), :4184-4188 (court order), :5106-5109 (commit)` _(root: greedy-allocation, RECURRING)_
  - The fill loop is `for (let index = 0; index < effectiveCount; index += 1)` where each iteration calls `suggestNextMatch` ONCE (line 4375) for a single court, then permanently commits that court's 4 players to `batchBusyIds` (5108-5109) before the next court is solved. Courts are chosen in ascending index order (`openCourtIdxs.find(idx => !roundCourtIdxs.has(idx)) ?? openCourtIdxs[0]`, 4186), never worst-pool-first. Consequence: whichever court fills LAST inherits whatever pool the earlier courts left behind. If the earlier (greedy-optimal for themselves) courts skim off the mid-skill players, the leftover pool for the last court is bimodal (a few very-low + a few very-high) OR stale (only players who already met) -> the last court is FORCED into a blowout or a 3rd meeting, and if the residue is <4 players the court is left empty (stuck). All three recurring symptoms are the same event: a depleted leftover partition for the last-filled court. This is proven by the fact that every downstream repair is a post-hoc patch on this partition rather than a re-partition.
- **[high] Post-pass repairs are ~16 independent local optimizers with conflicting objective functions and per-metric guards — they cannot converge** — `live-preview.ts:2883-2968 (swap 2-opt), :2415-2431 (repeat objective), :2433-2477 (participation objective), :5208-5250 (repair chain)` _(root: fairness-conflict, RECURRING)_
  - After the greedy fill, quality is patched by a SEQUENCE of local optimizers each defending a DIFFERENT metric with its OWN scoring function and thresholds: repairSuggestedPayloadBatch does a PVNA-first 2-opt swap (objective = maxPvna, 2913-2919) that only bounds repeat as a side constraint; repairAllIdlePayloadBatchParticipation optimizes a participation comparator (2479+); repairPayloadBatchRepeatExposure/...SevereRepeatFromPool optimize scorePayloadRepeatRepair (weights 1000/180/120/60... at 2417-2430); repairPayloadBatchBlowoutFromPool optimizes a blowout cost (2810). There is NO unified objective — each pass hill-climbs its own metric within hard bounds on the others, run in a fixed order (swap -> early-quality -> repeat-exposure -> participation -> severe-repeat-pool -> blowout-pool, 5208-5242). Because they are separate local searches over a partition they cannot re-cut, tightening any one pass's threshold just relocates the failure to whichever metric the NEXT reported case happens to hit. This is the mechanical reason point-fixes keep spawning new cases: you are moving the boundary between local optima, not enlarging the reachable solution set.
